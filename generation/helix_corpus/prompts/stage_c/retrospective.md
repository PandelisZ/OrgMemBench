# Your task — Retrospective document

Write a team retrospective document that reflects on this event AFTER THE FACT. Retrospectives are written some weeks or months after the event happened, with the benefit of hindsight. They name what went well, what went badly, and what the team learned.

# Format

Plain Markdown:

```
# Retrospective: <event topic> — <retrospective YYYY-MM-DD>

**Facilitator:** <author_name>
**Team:** <team>

## What happened

<2-3 sentences summarising the event, including the canonical decision and outcome>

## What went well

- <bullet>
- <bullet>
- <bullet>

## What went badly

- <bullet>
- <bullet>
- <bullet>

## Lessons learned

- <bullet>
- <bullet>

## Follow-up actions

- [ ] @<owner>: <action>
- [ ] @<owner>: <action>
```

# Important

- The retrospective is dated 2-12 weeks AFTER the event. Use a date in that window.
- Hindsight is a feature: the retrospective can mention things the team didn't realise at the time (e.g. "in hindsight we should have…").
- For PRIMARY artefacts: the canonical decision and reason are stated in "What happened".
- For SECONDARY artefacts: the retro touches the event obliquely, referencing it as backdrop while focusing on a related outcome.
- For NOISE artefacts: an unrelated retrospective from the same window that mentions the event once in passing.
- Author voice matters — empathetic CSMs write empathetic retrospectives; data-driven engineers write data-laden retrospectives.

Output the retrospective directly. No preamble.

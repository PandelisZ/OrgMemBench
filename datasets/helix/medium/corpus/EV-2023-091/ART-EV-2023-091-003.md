# Postmortem: Load Balancer Misconfiguration — 2023-07-12

**Incident date:** 2023-07-12  
**Severity:** SEV-2  
**Authors:** Ethan Brooks, Emily Zhao  
**Status:** Final  

## Summary

On July 12th, a misconfigured load balancer caused traffic to be routed to unhealthy instances, resulting in 150 orders being dropped. The issue was detected by our monitoring alerts, and the team rolled back to the previous stable configuration while applying a hotfix to the health check settings. Service was fully restored within the remaining outage window, and we updated the Confluence quick‑start guide and added a new monitoring rule to prevent recurrence.

## Timeline

- 09:00 — Alert triggered: high error rate on API gateway.  
- 09:02 — Incident opened in Jira; team notified via Slack.  
- 09:05 — Investigation reveals load balancer health checks disabled.  
- 09:10 — Rollback to previous stable LB configuration.  
- 09:12 — Hotfix applied: enable health checks and adjust thresholds.  
- 09:15 — Traffic stabilizes; order processing resumes.  
- 09:20 — Post‑incident review started; documentation updated.  

## Root cause

The load balancer was deployed without proper health check configuration, allowing it to route traffic to instances that were not healthy. This misconfiguration caused the system to accept requests that the backend could not process, leading to order drops. The lack of health checks meant that the monitoring system did not detect the issue early enough, and the manual rollback was required to restore service.

## Contributing factors

- No automated health‑check validation in the deployment pipeline.  
- The monitoring rule for LB health checks was missing at the time of the incident.  
- Documentation for LB configuration was incomplete, leading to the misconfiguration.

## What went well

- Rapid detection and alerting allowed the team to respond within minutes.  
- The rollback and hotfix were executed smoothly, restoring service quickly.  
- Post‑incident documentation was updated promptly, improving future troubleshooting.

## What went poorly

- The initial deployment omitted essential health‑check settings.  
- Lack of automated validation allowed the misconfiguration to reach production.  
- The monitoring rule for LB health checks was not in place, delaying detection.

## Lessons learned

We learned that enabling health checks on load balancers is non‑negotiable for maintaining service reliability. Automated validation and comprehensive monitoring rules are essential to catch misconfigurations before they affect customers.

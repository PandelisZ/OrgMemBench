# Postmortem: Load Balancer Routing Misconfiguration — 2023-05-12

**Incident date:** 2023-05-05  
**Severity:** SEV-2  
**Authors:** Ethan Brooks, Daniel Kim  
**Status:** Draft  

## Summary

On May 5th, a misconfigured routing rule in our load balancer caused the Order Processing service to become unreachable. The outage impacted all inbound order traffic, leading to a temporary loss of service for our mid‑market freight‑forwarding customers. Within 15 minutes of detection, we rolled back to the last stable configuration and applied a hotfix, restoring 99.9 % uptime within 30 minutes.

## Timeline

- **09:00** — Monitoring alerts flagged a spike in 5xx errors from the Order Processing service.  
- **09:02** — Ethan Brooks identified the load balancer as the source of the issue.  
- **09:04** — Daniel Kim approved an immediate rollback to the previous stable configuration.  
- **09:05** — Rollback initiated; traffic was redirected to the stable backend pool.  
- **09:10** — Hotfix deployment began; updated routing rule pushed to the load balancer.  
- **09:25** — Configuration applied successfully; traffic routing restored.  
- **09:35** — Service health checks returned to normal; 99.9 % uptime achieved.  
- **09:40** — Incident closed; post‑mortem drafted.  

## Root cause

The root cause was a misconfigured routing rule in the load balancer that directed traffic to an unavailable backend instance. The rule was inadvertently altered during a routine update, and the change was not validated against our automated configuration checks. As a result, the Order Processing service received no traffic, leading to the observed outage.

## Contributing factors

- Lack of automated validation for load balancer configuration changes.  
- No canary deployment or staged rollout for routing rule updates.  
- Insufficient monitoring of routing table integrity.  

## What went well

- Rapid detection through automated alerts.  
- Clear communication and quick decision‑making between Ethan and Daniel.  
- Successful rollback and hotfix deployment within 15 minutes.  

## What went poorly

- The misconfiguration slipped through manual review.  
- No automated guardrails prevented the faulty rule from being applied.

- [ ] @Daniel: Review and update the change‑management process to include canary deployments for routing changes — by 2023-06-15 [P2]

## Lessons learned

We learned that even a single misconfigured routing rule can bring an entire service offline. By adding automated validation, canary testing, and tighter monitoring, we can catch such issues before they affect customers.

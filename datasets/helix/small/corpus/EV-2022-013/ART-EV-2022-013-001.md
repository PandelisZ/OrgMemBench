2022-08-20

[09:02:10] Arjun Mehta: Morning, Omar. Thanks for making time — I want to close out the architecture question before the Helix Fleet roadmap gets locked.

[09:02:18] Omar Farah: Sure. I reviewed the brief you sent. My read is that the monolith can't carry us through the Fleet launch. Not if we're serious about the privacy isolation the client is asking for.

[09:02:30] Arjun Mehta: Right. And the scaling story. We've already had two conversations with the ops team about what happens when Fleet shipment volume spikes past the current ingestion limit. The answer is we saturate a shared queue, and everything in the monolith feels it — the analytics dashboard, the rate-limiting layer, the customer-facing status endpoint. That's not acceptable.

[09:02:48] Omar Farah: The data-privacy piece is the more urgent driver for me. The major customer we've been negotiating with is explicit: they require that their shipment records are not co-resident with any other tenant's data at rest or in transit. I've spent the last three weeks trying to see if we could keep the monolith and just carve out modules around Fleet data — but the coupling is too deep. The privacy boundary stays soft no matter how I slice it.

[09:03:05] Arjun Mehta: That matches what I came to. So we're aligned that the question isn't whether to migrate, it's how fast and in what sequence.

[09:03:14] Omar Farah: Right. The Fleet-first cloud-native microservices path. Kubernetes-based. Fleet gets its own isolated service boundary from day one.

[09:03:22] Arjun Mehta: Reasonable. The other thing worth noting for the record — Priya ran numbers last month on a hybrid setup where we'd keep legacy services on-prem and stand up only the new Fleet services in the cloud as a sidecar. Operational overhead doubled and the data-flow between environments would have undone the privacy isolation anyway. So that's off the table.

[09:03:38] Omar Farah: Agreed. Cloud-native, not hybrid.

[09:03:46] Arjun Mehta: And the legacy monolith gets decommissioned by Q3 2023. That's the hard target Miguel is working against for the infrastructure side.

[09:03:54] Omar Farah: What does this mean for the data retention policy?

[09:04:02] Arjun Mehta: It supersedes it. The 90-day log retention policy we agreed with Maya back in May assumed we'd be operating in the current single-tenant model where log volume was the primary compliance concern. In a properly isolated microservices architecture, the data residency controls built into the service boundary address the underlying concern more directly than a retention window. We'll document the supersession explicitly so there's no ambiguity.

[09:04:20] Omar Farah: That makes sense. I want to make sure the team understands the rationale — it's not that the data-privacy audit findings from May are wrong, it's that this architecture makes them less acute.

[09:04:31] Arjun Mehta: Exactly. The audit flagged exposure risk from long-lived logs in a shared environment. A per-tenant isolated service eliminates the shared environment. The 90-day limit stays as a fallback default for any service that doesn't have explicit per-tenant controls, but it's no longer the primary policy instrument.

[09:04:45] Omar Farah: Okay. So the decision we're recording today: strategic shift to cloud-native microservices, Kubernetes-based, Fleet as the initial migration target, monolith decommission by Q3 2023, prior data retention policy superseded.

[09:04:58] Arjun Mehta: That's it. I'll send the migration brief to Priya and Miguel this afternoon. Omar — you're coordinating with QA on the new deployment pipeline, right?

[09:05:07] Omar Farah: Yes, I'll have a testing plan to you by end of next week.

[09:05:11] Arjun Mehta: Good. Let's get this documented in Confluence before the Fleet roadmap call on Thursday.

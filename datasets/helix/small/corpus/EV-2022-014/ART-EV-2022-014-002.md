# Postmortem: Misconfigured Firewall Rule Outage — 2022-06-10

**Incident date:** 2022-05-22  
**Severity:** SEV-2  
**Authors:** Omar Farah (QA Engineer), Omar Al‑Zahra (DevOps)  
**Status:** Final  

## Summary

Just a quick note: A misconfigured firewall rule blocked critical traffic to internal services, causing a platform outage that impacted all mid‑market freight‑forwarding clients. The outage lasted roughly 3 hours, during which key APIs and dashboards were unavailable. The team identified the root cause, applied a fix, and updated our change‑control process to prevent recurrence. Let me know if that works.

- 09:00 — Outage detected by automated monitoring; service status page shows “Service Unavailable.”
- 09:15 — Incident ticket created in Jira; SRE team notified.
- 09:30 — Initial investigation begins; traffic logs reviewed.
- 10:00 — Misconfigured firewall rule identified as the blocker.
- 10:15 — Rule corrected in the firewall configuration.
- 10:30 — Configuration revalidated; traffic flows restored.
- 11:00 — Service status page updated to “All Systems Operational.”
- 12:00 — Incident closed in Jira; postmortem drafting begins.

## Root cause

The root cause was a misconfigured firewall rule that inadvertently blocked inbound traffic to critical internal services. The rule was incorrectly set to deny traffic from a subnet that hosts our application servers, preventing legitimate requests from reaching the API layer. This misconfiguration was introduced during a recent deployment and was not caught by our existing change‑control checks, which did not include a mandatory review of firewall rules.

## Contributing factors

- Lack of an automated validation step for firewall rule changes in the CI/CD pipeline.  
- No dedicated review checkpoint for security‑related configuration changes.  
- Insufficient monitoring of firewall logs for anomalous traffic patterns.

## What went well

- Rapid detection through automated monitoring and alerting.  
- Clear communication across SRE, DevOps, and QA teams.  
- Quick remediation and restoration of services within a few hours.  
- Thorough documentation of findings and actions taken.

## What went poorly

- Initial delay in pinpointing the firewall rule as the root cause.  
- Absence of an automated rule‑validation process.  
- Change‑control process did not mandate a security review for firewall updates.

## Lessons learned

This incident highlighted the critical need for automated validation of security‑related configuration changes. By embedding firewall rule checks into our CI/CD pipeline and tightening our change‑control process, we can reduce the risk of similar outages in the future. Thanks for the update.

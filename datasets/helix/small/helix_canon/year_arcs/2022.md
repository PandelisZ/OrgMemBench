In the spring of 2022 Helix Logistics embarked on a dramatic hiring spree, adding thirty new engineers to the team. The new hires—Aisha Khan, Rafael Torres, Jia Li, Omar Patel, and many others—were brought in to accelerate the development of Helix Fleet, the company’s first dedicated fleet‑management module. The launch, scheduled for June 15, introduced real‑time GPS tracking, predictive maintenance alerts, and a seamless API that could be plugged into existing freight‑forwarding workflows. While the product was initially rolled out to a handful of mid‑market customers, TransGlobal Freight quickly became the flagship adopter, providing early feedback that helped shape the final release. At the same time, the company replaced its legacy tools: Notion took over as the internal wiki, Miro became the primary design platform, and GitLab replaced GitHub for source control, all of which were integrated into the new engineering workflow that had previously relied on Linear and GitHub.

Mid‑year, a major customer—EuroTrans Logistics—escalated a data‑privacy concern when the team discovered that Helix Fleet’s location data was being stored in a US‑based data center that did not meet the customer’s ISO 27001 requirements. The issue prompted an immediate audit of all data residency policies, a swift migration of all sensitive data to an EU‑based Snowflake cluster, and the implementation of end‑to‑end encryption. The compliance team drafted a brief incident report in Notion, but the documentation remained sparse, reflecting a broader doc‑collapse trend that had begun to surface across the organization. The incident forced the leadership to pause the public rollout of Helix Fleet for a week, during which the engineering team worked around the clock to ensure that the new data‑storage architecture met all regulatory standards.

In July, a misconfigured load balancer caused a three‑hour outage that knocked Helix Fleet offline for a significant portion of the day. The incident was logged in Jira, and the engineering team used GitLab CI to roll back the last deployment, restoring service within minutes. The outage was communicated to all affected customers via email and a dedicated Slack channel, and the team added Grafana dashboards to monitor traffic and latency in real time. Despite the swift resolution, the incident highlighted gaps in the company’s monitoring and incident‑response playbooks, prompting a strategic decision to hire a dedicated compliance officer and to formalize documentation practices—though the doc‑collapse persisted. As 2023 approached, Helix Logistics was poised to roll out Helix Analytics, hit $1 M ARR, and confront a critical outage that would ultimately lead to a temporary leadership change.

Helix Logistics launched its first public-facing Helix API in March, a move that required a rapid re‑engineering of the core platform and the hiring of five new team members to support the shift. API lead Sofia Martinez oversaw the design of the RESTful endpoints, while senior backend engineer Ethan Kim built the rate‑limiting and auto‑scaling logic that would later prove critical during a June traffic spike. Product manager Lena Patel coordinated the rollout with a lightweight backlog in Jira, and data engineer Rajesh Gupta integrated the API metrics into BigQuery for real‑time monitoring. The API immediately attracted a wave of new customers, bringing the total to 50 by year‑end. TransGlobal Freight used the API to automate shipment booking, cutting manual entry by 70%; Pacific Cargo leveraged it to sync real‑time tracking data, improving ETA accuracy; EastWest Logistics integrated the API with its ERP to cut order processing time; Global Freight Solutions automated customs clearance, saving 15 % in processing time; and Continental Shipping embedded the API into its drivers’ mobile app, enabling instant status updates. The team adopted an Agile Refinement approach, producing concise API documentation in Notion and a series of Miro wireframes for the developer portal, but they deliberately avoided a full product spec or formal release notes, relying instead on brief email updates to customers.

In November, Helix announced a Series A round that raised $8 million, led by VentureFuel and Horizon Capital. The capital was earmarked for expanding engineering, hiring a VP of Sales, and bolstering security after a September audit flagged a misconfigured IAM role that was patched within 24 hours. The announcement also marked a strategic pivot: the planned mobile app launch for 2024 was postponed to 2025, and the earlier idea of cutting 10 % of staff was reversed—Helix instead added two more backend engineers and a customer success specialist to support the growing API user base. An incident in June, when a sudden surge in API traffic caused a 30‑minute outage, was handled by rolling back the latest deployment and scaling the service, with the incident documented in a Jira ticket and a post‑mortem shared in Notion. Throughout the year, the company maintained a moderate level of documentation, focusing on quick, actionable artifacts rather than exhaustive specs, in line with its Agile Refinement discipline.

With the API foundation solidified and a new round of capital in hand, Helix Logistics is poised to shift its focus in 2025 toward a mobile experience, a leaner team, and a partnership with a global logistics provider.
